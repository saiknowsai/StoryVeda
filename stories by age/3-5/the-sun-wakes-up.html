The Sun Wakes Up

A little girl learns the Gayatri mantra by watching her grandmother greet the sun every morning with love and gratitude.

Ages 3–6 📖 6 min read ✦ Gratitude ✦ Kindness
ॐ तत् सवितुर्वरेण्यं "O radiant light, we meditate on your divine glory." — Gayatri Mantra, Rigveda
Scene one

Before the birds wake up

Every morning, before the birds had finished their first song, Meera's grandmother was already awake.

Meera would hear soft footsteps in the dark, the gentle trickle of water from the kitchen, and then — silence. One morning, too curious to stay in bed, Meera crept out from under her warm blanket and followed.

She found her grandmother standing at the window. Eyes closed. Palms pressed together. Face turned toward the first pale light in the sky.

"Paati," Meera whispered. "What are you doing?"

Scene two

The sky changes colours

Her grandmother opened one eye and smiled. "Come. Stand beside me."

Meera padded over in her socks. Together they stood at the window and watched.

The sky turned from deep blue — to violet — to soft pink — to gold.

"Every morning," her grandmother said softly, "the sun wakes up and does his job. Without fail. Without complaint. He gives his light to everyone — rich and poor, young and old, kind and unkind. He asks for nothing back."

Meera thought about this. "Every single day?"

"Every single day. For longer than anyone can count."

The sky was fully golden now. Her grandmother took a slow breath. "We say thank you to him. That is all. Just — thank you."

Scene three

The mantra

Her grandmother took a slow breath and began — and the words came, soft and steady.

Meera didn't know the words yet. But they felt like something — like warm hands around a cup of tea. Like the feeling just after a long hug.

ॐ भूर्भुवः स्वः
तत्सवितुर्वरेण्यम्
भर्गो देवस्य धीमहि
धियो यो नः प्रचोदयात्॥

Om bhur bhuva swaha — Tat savitur varenyam — Bhargo devasya dhimahi — Dhiyo yo nah prachodayat

Scene four

What does it mean?

"Paati — what do the words mean?"

Her grandmother looked at her for a moment, then said gently:

"It means — O beautiful light, the light that made everything — please light up our minds too. Help us think clearly. Help us be good."

Meera looked out at the sun. It was fully up now, round and golden, pouring itself generously over rooftops, trees and puddles on the road.

"He does look kind," she said quietly.

Her grandmother laughed — a small, warm sound. "He is. And so can you be."

Scene five

Meera's own morning

From that day on, Meera woke up a little earlier. Not every day. But enough.

And on those mornings, standing at the window in her socks, she would say the words — slowly, stumbling sometimes, getting them a little wrong sometimes. But always sincerely.

Om bhur bhuva swaha...

And the sun, as always, showed up too.

The Gayatri Mantra appears in the Rigveda and is one of the oldest prayers in human history. It is a prayer not just for sunlight — but for the light of wisdom inside every heart.

✦ What we learn from this story

The sun gives without asking — and so can we

The sun gives his light every single day without being asked and without expecting anything in return. The Gayatri Mantra is simply our way of saying thank you — and asking for that same light to shine inside our own minds, so we too can be kind, clear and good.

🪔 Talk about it together

  • Why do you think Meera's grandmother thanks the sun every morning?
  • Can you think of something in your life that shows up for you every day without fail?
  • What do you think it means to have light inside your mind?
  • Would you like to learn the Gayatri Mantra together?
