The Boy Who Shared His Last Roti

A young boy named Arjun walked a long road with only one roti to his name. What he gave away came back — not as food, but as something far greater.

Ages 5–8 📖 8 min read ✦ Generosity ✦ Seva ✦ Gratitude
Scene one

The Long, Dusty Road

Arjun had been walking since morning.

His village was far behind him, and his grandmother's house was still far ahead. In his little bundle, he had just one thing left to eat — one roti, wrapped in a banana leaf, that his mother had packed with love.

"Save it for when you are very hungry," she had said.

Arjun patted the bundle. He was already hungry. But he kept walking.

Scene two

The Old Woman Under the Tree

Under a wide peepal tree, Arjun saw an old woman sitting alone. Her sari was dusty and her hands shook.

"Beta," she said softly, "I have not eaten since yesterday. My legs gave up on me here."

Arjun stopped.

He thought about his one roti. He thought about how hungry he was. He thought about how far he still had to go.

Then he opened his bundle.

"Please take it," he said. "My mother made it this morning. It is still a little warm."

Scene three

The Gift That Came Back

Arjun sat under the tree to rest his tired feet. His stomach growled. He had nothing left.

Then — plop! A ripe berry landed in his lap.

He looked up. A little squirrel sat on a branch, tilting its head at him.

Then another berry. And another.

Soon, there were berries enough for a meal. Sweet, juicy, and cool.

Arjun laughed. He did not know if the squirrel understood kindness. But somehow, it felt like the world was saying: well done.

Scene four

Grandmother's Door

Arjun reached his grandmother's house just as the stars were coming out.

She pulled him into a hug that smelled like cardamom and old stories.

"You look well, my child," she said, holding his face. "And happy."

"I am, Patti," Arjun said.

He did not tell her about the roti straight away. He just smiled.

Some things are too full to fit in words.

💬 Talk About It Together

  • 🌾 Arjun gave away his last roti. That's the hardest kind of sharing. Have you ever shared something you really didn't want to part with? How did it feel after?
  • 🐿️ Do you think the squirrel knew Arjun had been kind? Or was it just a coincidence? What do you believe?
  • 🙏 Seva means serving others without wanting anything back. Can you think of one small seva you could do today — at home, at school, or anywhere?
  • 🍃 Arjun's mother packed the roti "with love." What is something someone has given you that felt full of love, even if it was small?
